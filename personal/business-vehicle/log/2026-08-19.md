# 2026-08-19

Stood up the endeavor from an insurance shopping brief plus nine PDFs and four photographs of a dealer invoice. Worked as [[TCK-0002-business-vehicle-info-database]].

## What exists now

`data/vehicle.json` is the single source of truth. A Bun script in `scripts/` renders six pages into `notes/`, every one carrying a header that links the other five. Source documents are committed under `docs/`.

Tests cover the formatting helpers, the lease and warranty arithmetic, the nav builder, and the rendered output itself: each page must link all six, must not leak an unmasked account number, and must not right align a column of prose. 85 tests, lint and typecheck clean.

## What the documents said that the brief did not

Eight findings, all from reading the contracts against each other rather than one at a time.

The two that matter. Page 7 of the lease carries a signed corporate declaration of primarily business or commercial use, with the preauthorized payment marked business, while both insurance certificates rate the car personal with business kilometres blank. Section 3.4 of the Platinum AutoPlus agreement then excludes any vehicle used for business purposes from every service under it, which puts the $349 paid for theft compensation in doubt. Its 30 day cancellation window closed on 30 October 2025, so the only move left is to get an answer in writing from First Canadian Protection Products.

The lease permits a deductible of $2,500 or 5% of the vehicle price, whichever is greater. Five percent of $104,459.95 is $5,223, so the real ceiling is double what the Agreement to Furnish Insurance implies. Collision and comprehensive sit at $1,000 each and cost $3,401 combined on the renewal.

Section 13 already provides gap protection. That reframes the 5 Year New Vehicle Protection: it is not gap cover, it buys a new current year car instead of a cash settlement, and that is the argument against accepting plain OPCF 43R.

Front tyres read 4 mm on 12 August against a 3.2 mm lease return threshold, and tyres are excluded from the prepaid maintenance plan. The plan itself cost $1,779, exactly the sum of its three retail service prices, $480 plus $801 plus $498. It bought a fixed price rather than a discount.

One smaller point. The pricing worksheet says the warranty runs 4 years or 80,000 km while the dealer invoice prints an expiry of 29 September 2028.

On the 2025-26 certificate every optional accident benefit line reads Not included. On the renewal nine are itemised at $253. Both readings are defensible, so the pages state the facts rather than picking one: those benefits sat inside the mandatory Standard Benefits premium before 1 July 2026, and standard benefits fell only $28 while $253 appeared beside them.

## 2026-08-20, the market check

Added a seventh page answering whether the lease was a good deal, built on AutoTrader.ca comparables pulled 20 August 2026 rather than on the paperwork alone.

The structure is sound and the price was not. The 58% residual is generous: three year old GLC 43s ask $54,495 to $59,995 today, and the guaranteed buyout is $58,580, which sits at the top of that range and roughly $8,500 above likely wholesale. Mercedes-Benz Financial carries that risk, which is what a lease is for. Walk away at maturity.

Against that, the discount was 1.89%, $2,076 off a $109,929.95 list, and $5,107 went on soft products and dealer fees including a prepaid maintenance plan priced at exactly the sum of its three retail services. The kilometre tier is 18,000 a year against 15,871 actually driven, and unused kilometres are never credited.

The payoff today is about $91,460 against a comparable asking $83,000 and a trade near $73,000. That gap is normal at eleven months and is only a problem if the lease is terminated early, which section 16 makes expensive.

One correction worth recording: the 3.99% lease rate I first found for a 2025 GLC 43 came from a dealer in Ontario, California, not Ontario, Canada. Canadian rates had to be sourced separately, and a base 2026 GLC leases here at 3.99% with AMG carrying a premium, which makes the 4.49% signed reasonable rather than high.
