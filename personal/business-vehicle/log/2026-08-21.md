## Revising the position on the depreciation endorsement

The vault has been treating any OPCF 43 variant as a straight downgrade from the $794 endorsement. That reading needs qualifying.

Commonwell sets OPCF 43/43A to 36 months. From the 2025-09-30 in service date that runs to about September 2028, which is lease maturity. Duration is not the downgrade here. The difference is settlement basis: 43A waives depreciation against original price, the Desjardins endorsement replaces the car with a new current model year equivalent.

The car goes back at maturity. Section 13 of the lease already provides gap protection, so on a total loss the exposure is the MBFS payoff, not the cost of replacing a car in which there is no equity. The $794 endorsement mostly protects an owner's position, and there is no owner's position to protect. That is an argument for pressing Desjardins on whether $794 is worth paying, not an argument for taking Commonwell at $826 more.

## Six things missing from the quote

The All Perils deductible. The DCPD deductible. What PAK6 covers for $90. Whether OPCF 20 and OPCF 27 sit inside PAK6, because neither appears in Commonwell's endorsement column while both appear in the two losing columns. The #44 limit, shown as $2,000,000 in columns 2 and 3 and blank in column 1. And whether Commonwell will write the corporation as named insured with MBFS as loss payee at this premium.

Loss of use is the only place a broker has beaten the renewal so far. Desjardins carries OPCF 20 at $1,500, SGI offered $3,000 for $50. If PAK6 holds a higher limit that is the one genuinely better thing in this quote.
