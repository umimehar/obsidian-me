## What the email did not say

Juan compared the two quotes against each other. He does not have the Desjardins number, which is the withholding decision from 19 August working as intended, so his "$1,643 less than Economical" is the better of two losing options rather than a saving.

Economical's physical damage line is $7,022 at a $2,000 deductible against Desjardins at $3,401 for a $1,000 deductible. That single line is more than the whole spread between the two quotes. Juan wrote that most of the extra premium comes from the physical damage portion without saying how far out of line it is.

SGI only closes the gap by buying a $2,500 deductible. Priced back at $1,000 it moves up, not down. As quoted it costs $1,874 more a year and $1,500 more out of pocket on every claim.

The SGI OPCF 43 at $57 is listed as a pro in the email. It is not the same product as the $794 endorsement on the renewal, which runs 60 months and replaces the car with a new current model year equivalent rather than waiving depreciation against the original price. Twelve fewer months and a different settlement.

Neither quote is firm. The SGI PDF carries three underwriting messages: retrieve MVR prior to issuance, verify DASH and AutoPlus details and re-order, MVR required. With the minor conviction sitting on the 2026-27 certificate and the opening broker emails saying no convictions ever, the re-order moves that number up. Economical adds two of its own, that all modifications must be declared before binding and that prior unrepaired damage may make the vehicle ineligible.

Both quotes are rated personal use, which leaves the page 7 declared use conflict exactly where it was. Both carry OPCF 5 at no charge, so the lessee side is covered, but neither shows Mercedes-Benz Financial as loss payee.

## The one place the brokers win

Loss of use. The renewal carries OPCF 20 at $1,500. SGI offers $3,000 for $50 and Economical $5,000 for $115. That is a real gap in the current policy and it is cheap to close, so ask Desjardins to price $3,000 and $5,000.

## Decision

Keep Desjardins. Nothing gets cancelled.

Reversed the withholding decision from 19 August and sent Juan the renewal in full: $9,657 a year, the $1,000 deductibles, the 60 month replacement endorsement and the endorsement schedule. The original reasoning was that a hidden number gets quotes on merit rather than ones priced to land just under it. That only holds while the broker is still in range. At $1,874 and $3,517 over, isure is not, so a number to beat is worth more than a blind second attempt. Asked for SGI repriced at $1,000 All Perils, and said plainly that if none of his markets can beat $9,657 on that coverage I would rather hear it now than in late September.

Left out of the email deliberately: the loss payee gap, the personal use rating, and the missing OPCF 35. All three only matter if he comes back under $9,657. Raise them then.

## Business use settled at 95%

Owner confirmed on 20 August: the vehicle runs about 95% business, and that is the figure going to CRA. q6 closes. The lease declaration on page 7 was correct all along and both Desjardins certificates are wrong, rated personal with business kilometres blank.

The initial instinct was to keep the CRA position at 95% and leave the insurance rated personal, on the basis that the insurance side did not matter. It matters more than the tax side. Two records that contradict each other on the same vehicle, and the one that fails is a denied claim on a $104,460 car rather than a reassessment. Declared business use in Ontario is a personal auto rating class for a company car driven to client sites, not commercial auto, so the correct declaration is a surcharge rather than a different product.

Three consequences, and none of them are small.

Every premium on this file is rated on a use basis that no longer applies. That includes the $9,657 Desjardins renewal. The comparison written up above is still arithmetically right but the baseline is about to move, so ask Desjardins what business use does to $9,657 before 1 October rather than assuming the renewal is the safe option.

Section 3.4 of the Platinum AutoPlus agreement excludes any vehicle used for business purposes from every service under it. At 95% that is no longer arguable. The $349 is likely dead, and the 30 day cancellation window closed on 30 October 2025, so the only move is still a written answer from First Canadian Protection Products.

No mileage log exists. CRA asks to see a 95% claim supported, and this is now the weakest part of the position. Start one and reconstruct back to 2025-09-30 where possible.
