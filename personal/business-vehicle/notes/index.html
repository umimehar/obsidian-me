What the documents disagree about

Every one of these came out of reading the contracts against each other.

The lease declares business use, the insurance is rated personal

high

Page 7 of the lease carries a signed corporate declaration: the vehicle will be used primarily for business or commercial purposes. The preauthorized payment authorisation on the same page is ticked business purposes. Both insurance certificates rate the car as personal use, with the business kilometre field blank and the payment schedule marked Personal.

Why it matters. An insurer that finds the declared use wrong can deny a claim on the whole policy, not just adjust the premium. This is the largest uninsured exposure on the file and it costs nothing to fix.

Do this. Settle the real business use percentage, then correct the rating with Desjardins or disclose it to the brokers before anyone binds.

Platinum Security may already be void for the same reason

high

Section 3.4 of the Platinum AutoPlus agreement makes a vehicle used for commercial or business purposes an Ineligible Vehicle, with no entitlement to any service. The lease declares exactly that use.

Why it matters. $349 was paid for theft compensation worth up to $7,000 that may not be payable. The 30 day cancellation window closed on 30 October 2025, so the only move left is to establish in writing whether the agreement stands.

Do this. Put the question to First Canadian Protection Products at 1-800-381-2580 in writing, quoting agreement AP-12876238.

The deductible ceiling is about $5,223, not $2,500

medium

The lease requires a maximum deductible of $2,500 or 5% of the price of the vehicle, whichever is greater. Five percent of the $104,459.95 leased vehicle amount is $5,223. The separate Agreement to Furnish Insurance drops the tie breaker and reads as though $2,500 were the cap.

Why it matters. Collision and comprehensive sit at $1,000 each, costing $3,401 combined on the renewal. There is contractual room to take a much higher deductible if a quote prices it well.

Do this. Ask each broker to price collision and comprehensive at $2,500 alongside the $1,000 they will quote by default.

The lease already carries gap protection

medium

Section 13 of the lease provides gap protection as standard. On a total loss the insurance proceeds clear the lease balance and the only amounts still owed are the deductible, arrears, breach amounts and prorated excess kilometre charges. MBFS must be consulted before any actual cash value settlement is agreed, or the protection is forfeited.

Why it matters. It changes what the $794 5 Year New Vehicle Protection is actually buying. It is not gap cover. It buys a new current year replacement car instead of a cash settlement, which is a different and more valuable thing, and it is the reason a cheaper quote carrying plain OPCF 43R is a downgrade.

Do this. Do not let any broker sell separate gap or loan protection coverage. Ask instead whether their carrier offers true new vehicle replacement.

No certificate names MBFS as loss payee, which the lease requires

medium

Section 12 of the lease requires MBFS listed as Additional Named Insured and Loss Payee for comprehensive, fire, theft and collision, and as Additional Named Insured for liability. On both certificates the Lienholders box, headed to whom loss may be jointly payable, is blank. MBFS appears in the Lessor field and in the named insured block of the liability slips. OPCF 5 is on the policy, which extends coverage to the specified lessee.

Why it matters. Whether the Lessor field plus OPCF 5 satisfies a loss payee requirement is an insurer practice question, not something the certificate settles. If it does not, a physical damage cheque could be issued without MBFS on it, and section 12 makes a failure to maintain the required insurance an immediate default under the lease.

Do this. Ask Desjardins to confirm in writing that MBFS is recorded as loss payee for the physical damage coverages, and have it added if it is not. Put the same requirement to every broker as a condition of any replacement quote.

Front tyres are 0.8 mm above the lease return threshold

low

The 12 August 2026 inspection read 4 mm at both front wheels and 5 mm at the rear. The lease counts tyres with less than 3.2 mm tread remaining as excess wear and tear. Tyres are also excluded from the prepaid maintenance plan.

Why it matters. Replacement is at the lessee's expense either way, and summer performance tyres on 21 inch AMG wheels are not cheap. Worth planning rather than discovering at return.

Do this. Recheck the tread at the next service and budget for front tyres before lease maturity on 30 September 2028.

low
