Lazer left in May and MIR carried the year: $261,878 of revenue, $13,368 of tax, a $4,592 refund and no dividends.

The corporation

Lazer paid nine times before its contract ended on 2 May; MIR paid every two weeks all year. Revenue before HST was $261,878.

From January to April the corporation sent Umar $28,000. A draft resolution at year end called it reimbursement plus a small advance, but the filed T2 2025 went further: it counted $41,357 of business costs Umar had paid personally, so the $28,000 is fully repaid costs and the corporation still owed him $13,357 at year end. There were no dividends and no shareholder loan.

In September the corporation leased a Mercedes GLC 43 for 36 months at $1,750.01 a month. The T2 2025 deducted all Mercedes costs at 100% business use with no s.67.3 cap: about $6,608 above the cap, about $9,600 if the down payment is also spread over the 36 month term. Umar's own car, used until September, was paid mileage and also had its actual costs deducted, a likely double claim of about $3,270.

Maham joined the payroll in December with $16,000 of salary. Instalments of $17,960 beat the $13,368 of tax, so the return filed on 18 May 2026 brought a $4,592 refund. The HST return for 2024 to 2025 was filed on 24 September with $35,121.50 owing, and the $4,960.50 overpaid came back on 10 October.

Personal

Umar earned $142,011.53 as a Lazer employee (T4), with $46,867.74 of tax taken off. He put $15,000 into his RRSP, $8,000 into an FHSA and $25,000 into TFSAs.

His 2025 return, signed 20 April 2026, shows a refund of $22,130.37. Maham's return shows $16,000 from the corporation and a $40.17 refund. No notices of assessment are on file yet.
