## 5a. How the shareholder loan disappeared — the arithmetic

Confirmed by Umar Aug 3, 2026: *"he told me there is no shareholder loan anymore. He adjusted the amount, some in miscellaneous expenses and some in other expenses."* The filed numbers bear this out.

**The draft position.** The corporation paid Umar $28,000 in cash Jan–Apr 2025. If reimbursable personal-CC expenses were only $24,143.25 (40% rent) or $21,031.65 (30% rent), the excess would have been a loan **to** the shareholder:

| Reimbursement recognised | Excess = shareholder loan |
|---|---:|
| $24,143.25 (40% rent draft) | $3,856.75 |
| $21,031.65 (30% rent draft) | $6,968.35 |

**What was actually filed.** Schedule 100 line 2780 reports **$13,357 due TO the shareholder/director**. For the corporation to still owe Umar $13,357 after paying him $28,000, it must have recognised **$41,357** of expenses he funded personally:

```
28,000  cash already paid to Umar
+13,357  still owed at 31 Dec 2025 (line 2780)
=41,357  director-funded expenses recognised on S125
```

That is roughly $17,000 more than the 40% draft and $20,000 more than the 30% draft. Ejaz spread it across the catch-all and director-funded expense lines — his own words on the largest of them: *"It's a balancing number from all expenses when placed correctly to match with all payments through bank or both credit cards."*

| S125 line | GIFI | 2025 |
|---|---|---:|
| Other expenses | 9270 | $5,332 |
| Rental (home office) | 8910 | $9,335 |
| Vehicle expenses | 9281 | $24,315 |
| Telephone and telecommunications | 9225 | $1,789 |
| Insurance | 8690 | $1,866 |

**The point:** the $13,357 is not a figure that predates the adjustment — it is the *result* of it. Before: a loan owed by Umar. After: expenses recognised, and the corporation owing Umar $13,357. There is no shareholder loan anywhere in the filed return, and no ITA s.15(2) exposure.

**Audit defense:** the recognised expenses trace to `personal_cc_parsed.csv` (96 entries), `cc_v2_parsed.csv` (233 entries, receipts 100% on file), mileage logs, and the lease agreement supporting the home-office allocation. If CRA challenges the quantum, the fallback is a smaller reimbursement and a correspondingly smaller amount due to the director — a balance-sheet reclass, not additional tax.
