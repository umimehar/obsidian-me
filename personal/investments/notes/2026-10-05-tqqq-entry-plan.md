# TQQQ entry plan, TFSA (self directed), October 2026

Drafted 2026-10-05. Not yet executed. The TFSA is maxed for 2026, so every TQQQ dollar comes from selling QQC, which tracks the same Nasdaq 100 at 1x.

## Starting point

TFSA (self directed) on 2026-10-05, from the Wealthsimple screen: VFV $13,872.84, XEQT $9,816.73, QQC $9,338.83, CHPS $5,640.07, ZGLD $3,976.10. About $42,645 in total. QQC and CHPS together are 35% of the account.

TQQQ is the BetaPro 3x Nasdaq 100 Daily Leveraged Bull ETF on the TSX, CAD hedged, launched 2025-06-16. MER 1.44% plus a trading expense ratio of 4.15% as of 2026-06-30, so about 5.6% a year of drag. Price $40.95 on 2026-10-02.

## Market on the day of the decision

The Nasdaq 100 closed at a record of about 30,957 on 2026-10-02, up 22.4% for 2026. The rally came from a weak September jobs report (29,000 jobs against 90,000 expected), which cut the odds of an October Fed hike to 19.5% from 68.9% a week earlier. VIX was 15.31. Forward P/E on the Nasdaq 100 was about 22.4 on 2026-09-25, a little above its long run average.

Three dated events sit inside the next six weeks:

- 2026-10-21, Tesla earnings.
- 2026-10-28, Fed decision at 2 PM ET, plus Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta earnings.
- 2026-10-29, Amazon and Apple earnings.
- 2026-11-03, U.S. midterm elections.

Those five companies are a large share of the Nasdaq 100, and TQQQ moves three times whatever the index does on each of those days.

## Decision: staged entry, not all now and not waiting for a dip

Waiting for a dip is a bet that one comes before the index runs further, and a record high says nothing on its own about next month. Buying everything now puts the whole position in front of the Fed and the earnings week at 3x. Three tranches split the difference. If the market rallies, the later tranches cost more; that is the price of not betting everything on one day.

| Tranche | Date | Trade |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2026-10-07, midweek, off the 2026-10-05 high | sell $1,000 QQC, buy $1,000 TQQQ |
| 2 | 2026-10-30, after the Fed and big tech earnings | sell $1,000 QQC, buy $1,000 TQQQ |
| 3 | 2026-11-16, after the midterms | sell $1,000 QQC, buy $1,000 TQQQ |

End state: about $3,000 of TQQQ (7% of the account) and about $6,340 of QQC.

Execution: sell and buy on the same day so the money never sits as cash. Use limit orders, placed after 10:00 ET, not in the opening minutes when spreads are widest.

## Dip triggers

Measure against the Nasdaq 100's running closing high, not the 30,957 figure, because the high moves up if the rally continues.

- 10% below the high (about 27,860 today): buy every tranche not yet bought, the next trading day.
- 20% below the high (about 24,770 today): move $2,000 of VFV into TQQQ. Once only.

At a 20% drop TQQQ will be down 50% or more. That is when the rule exists to make the trade, so do not reopen the decision then.

## Limits

- TQQQ stays at or under 15% of the TFSA (about $6,400 at today's size). Check in the first week of January, April, July and October. Above 15%, sell back to 10% and put the proceeds into XEQT.
- No selling TQQQ because it fell. A 70% to 80% drawdown is part of owning it; the 2022 Nasdaq fall of about a third took the U.S. TQQQ down about 79%.
- A loss inside the TFSA is permanent room lost. $3,000 falling to $650 costs $2,350 of room that never comes back. That is the size this plan accepts.
- ZGLD stays. It is the gold exposure the 2026-10-01 plan relies on after GOLD was sold in Non-registered 2c62.

## From January 2027

New TFSA room opens on 2027-01-01. Add a TFSA line to the phase 3 payroll automations and put part of it into TQQQ on a recurring buy, subject to the 15% cap. Recurring buys average the entry price but do nothing about the daily reset decay or the 5.6% yearly cost, so the cap still governs.
