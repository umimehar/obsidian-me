## RRSP room now comes from the notice of assessment

The room bar was measuring against `CONTRIBUTION_LIMITS`, the generic CRA annual maximum ($33,810 for 2026). That ignores carry-forward, so it read as nearly full when there is plenty of room.

Added `ASSESSED_ROOM` in `analytics.ts` for personal room transcribed from an NOA, emitted as `assessed_room` on the ledger. `taxSummary` prefers it over the annual maximum and sets `assessed: true` on the row; `sections.ts` labels such a bar "assessed" and swaps the footnote, since the carry-forward caveat does not apply to it.

2025 NOA gives 2026 RRSP room of $70,752 (45,191 unused at the end of 2025 plus 25,561 earned in 2025). Against $33,000 contributed, $37,752 remains. Written up in [[rrsp-room]].

Five tests added: assessed room overriding the maximum, no leak into an adjacent year, and the `roomLimit` fallbacks. 117 pass.

## Filter scope now syncs to the query string

New `src/client/url.ts` owns the contract: `accts` (short_ids), `t`, `from`/`to`, `period`. Every param is omitted at its default, so a default view has a bare URL. Writes use `replaceState`, so Back still leaves the page in one press.

Checked the constraint that decided the design rather than assuming it: `history.replaceState` with a query string is permitted on a `file://` document in Chrome, so the query string works even though the page is opened from the vault as a local file. The call is still wrapped, so a stricter browser loses linkability and nothing else.

`decodeScope` is total, since a hand-edited URL still has to open. Verified in headless Chrome against the real built page: two accounts plus a preset restore correctly; a custom range plus `period=2026-05` reopens the May drill-down; a stale period is dropped and scrubbed; all-garbage params yield a clean default view.

16 tests added. Mutation-checked four of them (inverted range, empty-list guard, period validation, comma unescaping) — each mutation fails the suite, so they are not vacuous. 133 pass overall.

## Thirty year registered projection

Built end to end across seven tasks, four of them in parallel agents. Spec and plan went through two fable review passes before any code was written, which paid for itself.

Fable caught four real errors in my own documents. The worst was a generator bug in the fixture: it contributed $5,000 to the RESP in 2041, after the CESG lifetime was already exhausted in 2039, because grant room kept accruing with nothing decrementing it. No predicate could reproduce that row, so an engine coded to the written rule could never have passed its own regression baseline. It also found that `projectYears` had no way to receive the CRA constants I had said would ship on the ledger payload, and that the start-year CESG cap ignored grant already received that year.

Fixing the RESP rule changed the answer. Rather than a hardcoded rate schedule, the target is derived: contribute what claims all grant available that year, floored at $2,500, ceilinged at $5,000. The available catch-up turned out to be **$450**, not a multi-year $5,000/yr programme — claimable this year with $2,250 more. After that grant room never exceeds $500/yr, so a flat $5,000 would burn RESP room for nothing and forfeit about $1,700.

Two facts the spec insisted be derived were not reachable from the client payload at all: `GRANT` transactions (`ledger.flows` filters on `externalFlow()`, which never classifies GRANT) and account open dates. Both would have forced a hardcoded $550 and 2039. Added `series[].grant` and `accounts[].first_activity` to the ledger payload instead.

Mutation testing earned its keep twice. Three of the engine's four traps failed the suite when broken, but the start-year CESG cap showed zero failures — `grantRoom` and the annual cap both happen to equal $450 in the reference inputs, so removing either leaves the other binding. Added three tests forcing them apart. Separately, browser verification caught what unit tests could not: a `type=number` field blanks itself on non-numeric text and `Number("")` is 0, a legal rate here, so typing "abc" silently produced a 0% return instead of falling back.

Result at 8% return and 2% indexation: $1,788,782 contributed, $6,650 of further grant (lifetime $7,200), $128,732 withdrawn for a home in 2039, ending value $7,585,955, about $4,187,985 in today's money. All three statutory invariants land exactly on their caps.

192 tests pass. Verified in headless Chrome: 31 rows, four lifecycle notes, eight caveats, three chart series, rate inputs recomputing live and rejecting bad input, partial-selection warning, masking guard clean.
