Two things gate everything

Neither can be done for you, and both are slower than they look

Portal accounts. You cannot download a single document until you have one. BC Bid covers Qualicum Beach and BC Pension, Alberta Purchasing Connection covers Rocky Mountain House, and KPU runs its own Bonfire instance at kpu.bonfirehub.ca. Three registrations, all free, all needing company details. Do them first, today, before reading anything else on this page.

Insurance. You said the certificates are not in hand. Municipal RFPs ask for commercial general liability between $2M and $5M and professional liability, often $1M to $2M, and a broker takes days rather than hours for a first policy. Call one today. A bid that promises to obtain coverage on award is normally acceptable; a bid with no answer at all is not.

Rocky Mountain House closes 10:00 EDT on Friday 21 August, which is 08:00 in Alberta. Treat Thursday the 20th as the real deadline.

Qualicum Beach, AI semantic search strongest fit

Replacing keyword search on a town website and its document library with an AI semantic search solution, plus ongoing support. This is the outlier in the set. The scope is narrow enough that a small shop can own it end to end, and the subject matter is new enough that nobody bidding has ten years of municipal AI search references either. Where a redesign RFP scores you against agencies with fifty town websites behind them, this one scores you on technical approach. That is the ground you want to fight on. Redesign, development, migration, hosting and support, keeping the existing domain, over four months from 21 September. The scope is the most reusable thing in this list: win or lose, the proposal you write becomes the template for every small town redesign that follows, and those recur constantly. A negotiated RFP, which matters more than it sounds: terms are discussable after selection rather than fixed at submission, so a proposal that is strong on approach and imperfect on commercial detail still has a path. BC Pension Corporation not as prime

Twelve web properties across six brands, off Liferay, with a design system, managed hosting and Canadian data residency, over thirty six months. The notice is explicit that the winner keeps full contractual and delivery responsibility for everything including subcontracted work. On a first public sector bid with no comparable references, that is a losing hand played for weeks. Go at it as a named subcontractor to a prime instead, using the partnering route. The bid is worth writing only if a prime is already interested.

The references problem, and the honest way around it

Commercial projects with no public sector work is the single most common reason a first bid fails, and it fails at the compliance check rather than on merit. Three moves, in order of how much they buy you.

Ask your NDA clients for permission scoped to procurement. Most confidentiality clauses were written to stop marketing use, not to stop a named reference inside a confidential submission a buyer is legally required to keep closed. Ask for exactly that, in writing, and say why. A surprising number say yes, and it converts unnameable work into your strongest evidence.

Argue comparability rather than category. Requirements normally say "comparable projects", not "municipal projects". A commercial CMS migration with a content freeze, a redirect map and an accessibility audit is comparable to a town website redesign, and saying so explicitly, with the parallel drawn for the evaluator, is far better than hoping they infer it. Never claim public sector experience you do not have. Buyers verify, and getting caught ends more than one bid.

Take the first public reference as a subcontractor. That is what BC Pension is for. One delivered engagement under a prime turns this entire column green for every bid after it.

Rules that decide bids before anyone reads them

Public buyers run a compliance check first and score second. A submission that misses a mandatory item is set aside unread, however good the approach is. Late is late, with no discretion and no appeal, and portal clocks are the buyer's clock rather than yours. Questions close days before the bid does, so a question you think of while writing is usually too late to ask. Contacting anyone at the organisation outside the named contact can disqualify you outright during an open competition, which catches people who mean well. Any requested form must be submitted as the form, not as your own better version of it.

Then, after the award, read the notice naming the winner and the price. It is the only honest signal of what these buyers actually pay, and it is public.
