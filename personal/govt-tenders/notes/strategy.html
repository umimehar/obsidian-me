The search is 96% noise

Why browsing CanadaBuys by hand does not work

Filtering CanadaBuys to open service contracts matching the word "website" returns 242 notices. Ten of them are about a website. The search runs over the full notice text, so the other 232 matched boilerplate along the lines of "documents are available on our website" and are winter road maintenance, window cleaning, trip hazard removal and mosquito viral testing.

Ottawa does not tender websites

893 open federal notices, three touching web work

The federal open data export carried 893 open notices. Three touch the web at all: a Canada Revenue Agency secure online portal, a National Research Council content management system published as an advance contract award notice, which means the winner is already chosen, and an Export Development Canada user experience research contract.

Federal web work does not arrive as standalone RFPs. It flows through two supply arrangements, TBIPS (task based informatics professional services) and SBIPS (solution based). Both accept qualification submissions until 2028-07-04. Without one of them the federal market is functionally closed to you, and with one, work is drawn from the list rather than competed from scratch each time.

Honest arithmetic

Before committing weeks to this channel

Roughly five to ten live web tenders exist across the entire country at any moment. Each draws somewhere between eight and twenty bidders. Each gives three to six weeks and costs twenty to sixty unpaid hours of proposal writing. That is the whole visible market, and it does not grow because you check more often.

Scope also runs bigger than "a website". BC Pension wants twelve web properties across six brands moved off Liferay, with a design system, managed hosting and Canadian data residency, on a thirty six month contract. That is a platform program, not a build.

The gates matter more than the pitch. Municipal RFPs ask for three comparable references, $2M to $5M commercial general liability, WCB clearance, WCAG 2.1 AA experience and Canadian hosting. Missing any one disqualifies the bid on the compliance check, before a human reads your approach. Clear them once, in advance, not per bid.

Every notice carries a "businesses interested in partnering" list. One company had already added itself to the BC Pension RFP. Subcontracting under a prime is how you earn a citable public sector reference without carrying the insurance and bonding burden alone.

The play

In order

  1. Stop browsing CanadaBuys. The digest runs Monday, Wednesday and Friday and applies the title filter for you.
  2. Register on the portals that take submissions: BC Bid, Alberta Purchasing Connection, MERX, bids&tenders, Biddingo. Set saved search alerts on each. BC and Alberta produce most of the current flow.
  3. Clear the compliance gates now. Insurance, WCB, accessibility evidence, hosting story. Track them in tracking.md.
  4. Qualify for TBIPS and SBIPS. One qualification exercise, open until July 2028, and the only realistic federal route.
  5. Take the first reference as a subcontractor. Use the partnering lists rather than losing three bids on the references requirement.
  6. Pick one lane. Small town redesigns repeat constantly, run $40k to $150k, and reward a reusable proposal. Programs the size of BC Pension pay ten times that and need a team. Chasing both dilutes you.
  7. Read the award notices. Winner and price publish after the fact. It is the only honest signal of what these buyers actually pay.
